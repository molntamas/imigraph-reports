The same Python package as the tests, running in your browser through Pyodide (Python 3.14 in WebAssembly). The first load downloads Pyodide, numpy and scipy (about 15 MB) and may take half a minute.

Small things moving on a lattice graph: the playground (random walkers, lattice gas)

A rule is an edge rewrite: a walker edge {w,s} plus a chain of site edges on the left, the walker re-attached to a site of the chain on the right. Edit a rule in the drawing or in the text, both show the same rule. The rule set runs its ticked rules in order in every step. Choose the walker model, the collisions and the strategy for overlapping matches (see the explanation below the results), the starting universe (world size, walls, where the walkers begin, by clicking sites if you like), then Apply: the run is recomputed in Python at once, and the measured diffusion, drift and wall rate are compared with what the rules predict. Save result page writes everything into one self-contained HTML file.

2b. The rule of this run, explained

The space is a lattice graph; boundary nodes keep self-loops for the neighbours they lack (the walls). Each particle is a walker node attached by one edge. The rule picks, for every walker at once, labelled edges from its node with the rule's direction weights and moves the walker edge along the chain; a self-loop means the walker stays.

One walker on a 5×5 space; each application in four phases (state, match, choose, rewrite).

2e. Energy, momentum, entropy, walls, spreading

Energy and momentum are measured from every simulation step and averaged per recording interval; the ensemble values stay the same (temperature 2D/dt, momentum zero within its expected fluctuation) in both systems. The occupancy entropy rises towards ln(bins), on average but not monotonically.






Balls bouncing in a room: a graph gas held against the hard-disk gas




Several rigid balls, each one walker node (or the nodes within a radius), move in straight lines with integer velocities,
bounce elastically off the walls and collide with each other by a collision rule you choose. No gravity, no
friction. The same balls, from the same start, also run as a traditional hard-disk gas: that is the reference, and the
2D projection of the graph system is held against it frame by frame. Energy, momentum, entropy and the speed distribution
are tracked for both.
