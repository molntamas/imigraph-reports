imigraph live: Brownian motion in a box, imitated by a graph

The same Python package as the tests, running in your browser through Pyodide (Python 3.14 in WebAssembly). The first load downloads Pyodide, numpy and scipy (about 15 MB) and may take half a minute.

Small things moving on a lattice graph: the playground (random walkers, lattice gas, balls)

There is one kind of walker, the ball {w,s,v}: a walker node on site s with its ray v, the path of sites it hops through in one step (an empty ray o is a ball at rest, a random walker; a ray of one site is the walker of the lattice gas). The edges of the room carry no labels and no weights: walls are wall nodes, and what "straight on", "a turn" or "the same direction over there" means is read off the squares of the graph. A rule is a rewrite of ball hyperedges: the pattern on the left, what replaces it on the right, with annotations that say which edges are meant ({s,t}_k the edge of direction k at s, {s,wall}_k an edge onto a wall node, {v,v'}|k the mirror image of the ray across the axis of k; the explanation below the results lists them all). Motion, walls and collisions are all rules of this kind: Stream, Reflect, Exchange, Swap, Turn are in the presets and can be edited like any other rule. Edit a rule in the drawing or in the text, both show the same rule. The rule set runs its ticked rules in order in every step (rules with a hop label once per round of a step), so the order matters: Reflect and the collision rules go before Stream, which takes the hop. Choose the starting universe (world size, walls, where the balls begin, their velocities), then Apply: the run is recomputed in Python at once, and the measured diffusion, drift, wall rate, collisions, momentum and energy are shown next to what the rules predict. Save result page writes everything into one self-contained HTML file.

2b. The rule of this run, explained

The space is a lattice graph; boundary nodes keep self-loops for the neighbours they lack (the walls). Each particle is a walker node attached by one edge. The rule picks, for every walker at once, edges from its node uniformly and moves the walker edge along the chain; an edge onto a wall node means the walker stays.

One walker on a 5×5 space; each application in four phases (state, match, choose, rewrite).

2e. Energy, momentum, entropy, walls, spreading

Energy and momentum are measured from every simulation step and averaged per recording interval; the ensemble values stay the same (temperature 2D/dt, momentum zero within its expected fluctuation) in both systems. The occupancy entropy rises towards ln(bins), on average but not monotonically.






Balls bouncing in a room: a graph gas held against the hard-disk gas




Several balls, each one walker node (or the nodes within a radius), move in straight lines with integer velocities,
bounce elastically off the walls and collide with each other by a collision rule set you choose: a preset of the
rules above, or the playground's own ticked rule set. No gravity, no friction. The same balls, from the same start, also
run as a traditional hard-disk gas: that is the reference, and the 2D projection of the graph system is held against it
frame by frame. Energy, momentum, entropy and the speed distribution are tracked for both.
